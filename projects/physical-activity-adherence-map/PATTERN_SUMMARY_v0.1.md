# Pattern Summary — Micro Map v0.1

## Scope and limit
This is a **descriptive micro-map of 10 selected adult digital physical-activity intervention studies**, not a systematic review or meta-analysis. Patterns below describe this v0.1 set only; they are hypotheses for the next iteration, not estimates of general treatment effectiveness.

## 1. Self-monitoring + feedback is the common backbone
Across all 10 coded studies, some form of self-monitoring, behavioral feedback, activity display, diary, or monitoring-supported feedback appears in the intervention structure.

The delivery surface varies—smartphone app, wearable, website, SMS, accelerometer, or hybrid program—but the recurrent loop is:

`observe behavior → compare with a reference/goal → receive feedback/prompt → act again`

The meaningful design question therefore appears to be less “does the intervention contain tracking?” and more “what does the feedback loop do after measurement?”

## 2. Adding more digital contact does not reliably add more value
Several studies weaken the simple assumption that more reminders, more feedback, or more app exposure necessarily improves adherence:

- **PA006:** adding frequent SMS prompts to Fitbit self-monitoring produced an early advantage that did not persist to week 6.
- **PA009:** adding social-comparison feedback did not significantly outperform personal step feedback.
- **PA010:** continuing the app during the 6-month maintenance period did not outperform accelerometer-only maintenance.
- **PA004:** a relatively rich monitoring + tailored web-advice package produced no significant PA effect.

**Working hypothesis:** the marginal value of another digital component depends on whether it changes the behavioral decision loop, not merely whether it increases contact frequency.

## 3. Short-term response and maintenance are different problems
The map repeatedly shows time-dependent effects:

- **PA002:** enhanced Internet intervention differed from comparison at 3 months but not 6 months.
- **PA006:** SMS advantage appeared early and then disappeared.
- **PA007:** between-group step difference appeared at 12 weeks but not significantly at 24 weeks.
- **PA010:** initial 3-month activity gains were not further protected by prolonged app exposure.

This argues for coding **acquisition** and **maintenance** separately. A digital intervention that helps activity start is not automatically an adherence intervention that helps activity persist.

## 4. Relative advantage can conceal absolute decline or maintenance
Two studies make this especially clear:

- **PA003:** adaptive personalized goals performed better than a fixed 10,000-step goal, while absolute steps declined in both groups.
- **PA007:** the 12-week between-group difference largely reflected greater decline in the control group rather than a clear increase from baseline in the intervention group.

Therefore every future row should preserve:
`baseline direction + between-group contrast + time point`

A single label such as “effective” destroys important information.

## 5. Behavior outcomes and physiological outcomes must remain separate
**PA008** increased reported walking/leisure-time physical activity without improving peak VO₂ relative to usual care.

Digital behavior-change systems should therefore not be evaluated using an undifferentiated “health outcome” bucket. The pathway is at least:

`intervention exposure → behavior → sustained dose → physiological adaptation`

Failure at a later stage does not necessarily imply failure at an earlier stage.

## 6. Multi-component packages create an attribution problem
PA002, PA005, PA008, and PA010 combine several mechanisms. PA010 additionally combines digital components with in-person counseling.

These trials can tell us whether a **package** was associated with a behavioral difference, but they usually cannot identify which component caused it.

By contrast, factorial or component-comparison designs such as PA001 and PA009 are more useful for an eventual executable intervention map because they expose the incremental value of specific components.

## v0.1 synthesis
The 10-study map does **not** support a simple ranking such as “SMS works” or “apps work.”

It instead exposes a more useful design space:

`measurement → personal feedback → goal/reference → prompt/support → repeat`

with four unresolved questions:

1. Which reference works best: personal history, fixed goal, adaptive goal, or peer comparison?
2. Which components improve **maintenance**, not merely initial uptake?
3. When do prompts remain decision-relevant rather than becoming background noise?
4. Which effects belong to the digital component itself versus a multi-component package?

## Candidate question for v0.2
**Under what conditions does a digital self-monitoring + feedback loop remain behaviorally salient long enough to support physical-activity maintenance?**

That question is narrower and more testable than “Which digital physical-activity intervention works best?”
